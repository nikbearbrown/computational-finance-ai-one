# Defend the Model When the Attractive Answer Fails

The committee likes the account with the higher modeled expected payoff. Its mandate requires at least 980 next year in every specified state. One state misses by ten. A team member claims that the expected return makes up for the shortage. Your final model packet has to persuade an auditor, not a dashboard.

## Take the assignment before looking at the result

Open the [analyst model packet](../assessment/analyst-model-packet.md), record your unaided calculation, and choose Excel or Python to implement the same stated contract. Work with the synthetic data only. An agent may draft a formula or critique one unit convention after that first attempt. Neither tool is the authority to define the objective, make assumptions for the committee, or choose the final action.

All modeled terminal payoffs refer to one date; no trade happens during the period. There is 1,000 to allocate to combinations of the same one-year zero-coupon claim and synthetic equity exposure. The bond model is 105 after a year at a required 5% effective annual yield, worth 100 today. Equity model units cost 100 and pay 130 or 85 at the same terminal date. The cases use two explicitly weighted states, not a guarantee or a potential trading recommendation.

If the implementation reports about 95.24 for the bond, catch the misplaced exponent before multiplying holdings. Verify each candidate state payoff independently from the actual unit positions and verify that a funded order uses no more than the initial 1,000. Otherwise a bug can make the portfolio appear safe simply by buying imaginary units with the cash.

## Reconcile both choices

| Choice | Bond units | Equity units | High state | Low state | Equal-weight modeled mean |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 6 | 4 | 1,150 | 970 | 1,060 |
| B | 8 | 2 | 1,100 | 1,010 | 1,055 |

Within these choices, B is the only one meeting the terminal floor in both modeled states (the initial position also spends exactly 1,000). A has the larger modeled mean. The state floor is a condition on the action, not a descriptive statistic to trade away implicitly. Check that two opposed facts can be true at once. If the assumed bond payment fails or the horizon changes, the state table must be reopened. The test is defined only on the modeled scenarios.

A stress model that uses the lower equity outcome with the higher price of a new bond candidate must rebuild the unit count and horizon. Do not copy a result across a contract change. If both choices become infeasible, rejecting both is an available answer; do not prescribe an unapproved third portfolio.

**Teardown.** Present the committee with an output that has the higher mean correct and the binding scenario wrong. Explain how your plan would detect it even without the same software.

**Transfer.** Now set the lower equity payoff at 70 while keeping all other case inputs fixed. Recompute each choice's low outcome and the equal-weight expected result. Is the original state-floor recommendation still supported? Give the exact observation or input that would trigger a new validation round.
