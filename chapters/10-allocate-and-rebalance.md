# Allocate the Risky Budget, Then Rebalance the Holdings

A portfolio is labeled “60/40” months after its assets moved differently. The label records a target, not the live holdings. Meanwhile another presentation makes 70% of the whole account risky. Until you separate those weight layers, you won't know what the two investments represent in the account.

## Keep two weight systems explicit

Use the 40%-in-B model from the previous chapter, which has expected return 7% and base modeled variance .008704. Form a larger account with 70% of its value in that risky portfolio and 30% in a fictional one-period cash deposit. Assume the deposit returns a fixed 3% over the exact same period, with no default, early withdrawal, fees, taxes or reinvestment effects for the model. Treat these as hypothetical assumptions, not a cash rate quote or a safety recommendation.

Expected account return is `.70 × 7% + .30 × 3% = 5.8%`. Account variance is `.70² × .008704`, since the constant return contributes no variation in the stipulated comparison. Thus account standard deviation is about 6.53% on this model. The whole-account weights are 42% in A, 28% in B and 30% in the deposit. Check they sum to one. At the prior stress outcomes −14% and −20% for A and B, the whole-account return is −10.58% when the deposit indeed returns +3% on the matched date. This is a stress case, not a maximum possible loss.

You could have allocated using a target volatility instead. That would require trusting the risky portfolio's modeled variability and correlations across the full horizon. A required future expense is a different obligation. Choose constraints consistent with the reason the money was set aside.

## Rebalance using value, not old unit counts

In a separate scenario, a 1,000 risky subportfolio starts with 600 in A and 400 in B. A gains 20% and B loses 5%. Its end holdings are 720 and 380, total 1,100. The weights drift to roughly 65.45% and 34.55%. To restore 60/40 at the stated end value in a frictionless illustration, target 660 and 440. That calls for a 60 transfer from A to B, which changes exposures but does not itself create or destroy cash. In an actual comparison transaction costs, indivisibility, tax and execution may matter. This arithmetic is not a proposed trade on a live account.

**Teardown.** “Sell 6 percentage points of the original 1,000 because the target was 60% and today's screen says 66%.” The denominator changed and the proposed weight was rounded. Rebuild the transfer from current holding values and residuals.

**Transfer.** If costs of 5 in total are paid after the return and before rebalancing from the same risky account, the target would apply to 1,095, not 1,100. Compute the new target holdings. Identify which information is still missing to specify two settlement amounts and exactly where the fee comes from.
